# AAA Realistic Shadow System Implementation Specification

## Goal

Implement a physically plausible, production-quality shadow system capable of rendering realistic shadows for outdoor and indoor scenes with quality comparable to modern AAA engines.

---

## Phase 1: Light Classification

For every light source determine:

### Directional Light

* Sunlight
* Moonlight
* Large distant sources

Shadow Technique:

* Cascaded Shadow Maps (CSM)

### Spot Light

* Flashlights
* Lamps
* Vehicle headlights

Shadow Technique:

* High-resolution shadow map

### Point Light

* Bulbs
* Candles
* Explosions

Shadow Technique:

* Cubemap shadow maps

---

## Phase 2: Shadow Data Generation

### Directional Lights

Create 4-8 cascades.

Split camera frustum:

* Near
* Mid Near
* Mid Far
* Far

For each cascade:

1. Compute cascade frustum corners.
2. Transform corners into light space.
3. Calculate orthographic projection bounds.
4. Render depth-only pass.

Store:

* Depth map
* Cascade distance range
* Light matrix

Target Resolution:

* Cascade 0: 4096x4096
* Cascade 1: 4096x4096
* Cascade 2: 2048x2048
* Cascade 3: 2048x2048

---

## Phase 3: Physically Based Shadow Bias

Avoid constant bias.

Calculate bias using:

* Surface slope
* Receiver angle
* Light angle
* Depth gradient

Requirements:

* Eliminate shadow acne
* Eliminate peter-panning
* Stable under camera movement

---

## Phase 4: Contact Hardening Shadows

Implement Percentage Closer Soft Shadows (PCSS).

### Step 1

Search for blockers between:

Light → Receiver

### Step 2

Estimate penumbra size:

Penumbra Size ∝
Receiver Distance − Blocker Distance

### Step 3

Increase filter radius according to penumbra size.

Result:

* Sharp shadows near contact points
* Soft shadows farther away

This mimics real-world area lights.

---

## Phase 5: Temporal Stability

Prevent shimmering.

Requirements:

### Texel Snapping

Snap cascade projections to shadow texel size.

### Stable Cascades

Prevent projection movement from small camera changes.

### Temporal Filtering

Blend previous frame shadows.

Use:

* Motion vectors
* History rejection
* Variance clipping

---

## Phase 6: Area Light Shadows

Support:

* Rectangle lights
* Disc lights
* Tube lights

Technique:

* Ray traced shadows
  or
* Signed Distance Field shadows

Requirements:

* Soft penumbra
* Variable softness
* Accurate occlusion

---

## Phase 7: Shadow Denoising

For ray traced shadows:

1. Spatial filter
2. Bilateral filter
3. Temporal accumulation
4. Variance-guided denoiser

Preserve:

* Contact edges
* Fine geometry
* Small details

---

## Phase 8: Geometry Contribution

All geometry categories must participate.

### Static Meshes

Cast + receive shadows

### Skeletal Meshes

Cast + receive shadows

### Foliage

Cast + receive shadows

### Hair

Cast + receive shadows

### Particles

Optional shadow contribution

### Transparent Objects

Support:

* Alpha-tested shadows
* Dithered transparency shadows

---

## Phase 9: Indirect Shadowing

Implement ambient occlusion systems.

Combine:

* GTAO
* HBAO
* Ray Traced AO

Purpose:

Simulate indirect light blockage not captured by direct shadow maps.

---

## Phase 10: Volumetric Shadows

Render shadows inside:

* Fog
* Dust
* Smoke
* Atmosphere

Technique:

1. Volumetric lighting pass
2. Light-space shadow sampling
3. Temporal reprojection

Result:

Visible light shafts and god rays.

---

## Phase 11: Hybrid Ray Traced Shadows

For high-end hardware:

### Near Range

Ray traced shadows

### Medium Range

PCSS shadows

### Far Range

Cascaded shadow maps

Hybrid approach provides:

* Sharp contact shadows
* Realistic penumbra
* Good performance

---

## Phase 12: Physics-Based Light Behavior

Shadow softness depends on:

* Light source size
* Occluder distance
* Receiver distance

Never use arbitrary blur values.

Compute penumbra from geometric relationships:

Penumbra Width =
Light Radius ×
(Receiver Distance − Occluder Distance)
/
Occluder Distance

This produces physically plausible shadow growth.
